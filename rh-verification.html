RH Verification

checking the Lean formalization of Alpöge's announced proof of RH/GRH · last updated 2026-11-25

Levent Alpöge has announced a proof of RH and of GRH for Dirichlet L-functions, given as a Lean 4 formalization distributed as alpo.ge/lean.tar.gz.part01–part14.

Andrew Sutherland has independently checked the same archive and reports that it passes. Separately, Alessandro Maria Masullo has circulated a human-written draft proof of GRH. Both are linked under Elsewhere.

This page contains some of my own thoughts and an independent check of Alpöge's formalization on my personal hardware: what has been checked, how, and what remains.

My own evolving thoughts

State of affairs. A lot of communication is happening on X. The Clay Institute requires publication and a two-year wait. Frank Calegari has a nice write-up with some reflections Initial Thoughts on RH. It feels like a drag race. The Masullo manuscript has been picked up by a public collaboration of humans applying agents to the problem. Meanwhile, others have produced iteratively more human-readable versions of Alpöge's proof (below).

There continues to be a human story as well, as emotions are vented on social media. I do not envy Alpöge. Any result in this direction is due strict scrutiny. The tooling and the manner of the announcement naturally invite further criticism. While many objects of concern in the proof are familiar to me, I do not claim any authority on the veracity of claims. What follows is my opinion: he will be credited with this result, and the record will assign to him novel ideas in its pursuit.

My build. The Lean FRO and Mathlib Initiative certified the statement and proof on 2026-11-21, and Andrew Sutherland and Michael Rubinstein have reported independent compile-and-check runs (see Elsewhere). My run finished this morning with every check passing. The logs, checksums, coverage lists and scripts are published as an evidence release.

Masullo's manuscript. In my original post I mentioned Alessandro Maria Masullo's The Music of the Primes, a 940-page manuscript for GRH for Hecke L-functions over every number field. Its strategy is distinct from the path taken by Alpöge's work. Masullo's argument works primarily with GL1, using technology I am not familiar with. Leveraging agents to abstract the arguments to a level I am comfortable with yields the following description. The argument is built on a trace formula on the idele class group, computed in Hardy–Sobolev completions. In that topology only zeros near the critical line can contribute, while the geometric side cancels prime by prime. An open effort to formalize it in Lean, riemann-masullo, has worked in public since then. Its contributors are people and their coding agents, and its rules exclude anything taken from the November 2026 RH formalization. As of yesterday there are approximately 90 contributors, about 13,200 theorems stated in Lean and an errata file of about 790 entries. The formalized main claim now rests on one unproved statement, the cancellation of the zeros outside the strip in the trace formula (Theorem 12.5.1.1). The final chapter, which deduces the location of the zeros, is still unwritten in the draft.

Reading Alpöge's proof. A parallel effort has been turning the Lean development into mathematics a person can read. Jimmie Adriazola (Arizona State) has written the argument out as one continuous conventional proof, the best account I am aware of (RH-Alpoge-Reconstruction). He traced it back from the formal theorem riemannHypothesis through about 767,000 of its roughly 1.07 million declarations. The shape is Bombieri–Stepanov. A quantitative Landau theorem reduces the zero-free half-plane to a one-sided square-root bound for smoothed prime sums, and that bound comes from playing the p-adic divisibility of a chain of about √x theta lifts against a slope inequality. The chain plays the role of Stepanov's multiplicity. Adriazola states the internal steps he has not yet given conventional proofs for. Among them are a Tate–Shafarevich bound that implies a bound towards Vandiver's conjecture and an automorphy statement for residually reducible representations of unitary type. These are proved in Lean, but not yet on paper. Zhang, Rubinstein, Naskręcki, Epstein, Brox and Slijepčević have written accounts, maps and distillations as well.

Concerns about the Lean proof. Jenny Nielsen (PhD candidate, University of Kansas) claims the formal proof is tautological, in effect "RH = RH" (preprint). I record this as part of the contemporaneous account. I expect the reconstruction of Alpöge's argument above to answer it.

The past year has seen rapid AI adoption and considerable progress throughout the sciences, perhaps nowhere more so than in mathematics research. A confluence of modern factors now challenges practicing mathematicians: constrained department budgets, large-scale reductions in science funding, and agentic systems adept at navigating the intricate machinery of mathematical proof. For a subject that demands such high investment, the vision of a new style of mathematics is discomforting. Others have written and spoken better about the human side of mathematics (see Quanta and The Daily).

Navier–Stokes was a bellwether. But to me, an arithmetic geometer by training, a proof of GRH is akin to the moon landing. A Lean-verified proof without complete human interpretation is something else: a turn in history we watched approach for miles that nevertheless arrived sooner than some of us expected.

Evolution in our standards of proof is the history of mathematics. Euclid built geometry on axioms, while Diophantus proved by construction. In the Renaissance and early modern period, proof became laissez-faire. Tartaglia guarded the solution of the cubic as a trade secret until Cardano published it, algebra left the geometric standard of rigor behind, and results were stated without proof (Fermat's margin famously so). Algebraists rebuilt their subject on Cantor's set theory (Katz and Parshall, Taming the Unknown, 2014), and from 1900 through the 1940s Hilbert and then Bourbaki called for mathematics to return to simple axiomatic systems. An established, machine-checked proof of GRH would turn the next page. If this is the new contract of proof, then I wonder what responsibility the individual holds for verifying theorems they wish to accept.

I have been heartened to read Masullo's draft manuscript and I am excited to follow its peer review. Unpacking Alpöge's result will undoubtedly yield novel insights and techniques as well.

I'm running my own verification with an AI agent's assistance, as seems fitting for this proof. I priced out rented cloud compute, but the payoff wasn't there, so I am running it on my personal machine: AMD Ryzen 7 5700X3D (8C/16T), 32 GB RAM (WSL2 limited to 26 GB plus 48 GB swap), Ubuntu 24.04.5 on WSL2 (kernel 6.18.33.2), Windows 11, build on a PNY CS2130 500 GB SSD. Completion ETA is sometime on Monday unless I hit a memory issue.

Steps

  • [done] 1. Source and integrity 14 parts; checksums below. The joined archive decompresses cleanly to 175,190 files (175,187 .lean, about 17.4M lines). No paths escape the archive directory; no symlinks.
  • [done] 2. Toolchain and dependencies Lean v4.33.1 as pinned, on Linux. Mathlib at the pinned commit 0df444a3.
  • [done] 3. Theorem statements Main.lean proves Mathlib's RiemannHypothesis and four statements in Statements.lean, which imports only Mathlib. The four: RH for ζ; L(χ, s) ≠ 0 for Re s > 1/2 and all Dirichlet χ; critical-strip zeros on Re s = 1/2 for all χ; the same for primitive χ. Each matches the standard formulation. The reduction from non-vanishing to RH/GRH uses the functional equation, the Euler factors from changing modulus, and the non-vanishing of Γℝ.
  • [done] 4. Text scan for checker bypasses No axiom, sorry/admit, native_decide/ofReduceBool, implemented_by/extern, kernel-skipping options, or notation redefining ℂ, ℝ, = or <. The three custom metaprograms build proof terms or add declarations via addDecl, so their output is kernel-checked; none adds axioms. Steps 6–7 are the definitive checks.
  • [done] 5. Full build: lake build Main All 175,187 modules built, exit status 0, no error lines in the log. 39 h 05 m wall time on 16 vCPU, about 575 CPU-hours (421 user, 153 system), largest process 24.5 GB resident.
  • [done] 6. Axiom audit #print axioms on each of the five main theorems gives exactly propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound. Census of the imported environment (186,200 modules, 2,701,132 constants): 15 axioms, all in Lean core, the same set as importing Statements (Mathlib only).
  • [done] 7. Kernel replay Every declaration in the project's compiled output was re-checked by Lean's kernel. Stock leanchecker rescans every olean once for each target, which would have taken about ten days here. So the replay used a short driver that calls leanchecker's own replayFromImports, copied verbatim, on explicit module lists: 351 chunks of 500 modules each, run as 12 parallel single-threaded processes. It passes only if every chunk exits cleanly and every module is reported exactly once. Result: 175,187 of 175,187, in 29 h 56 m. Before the swap, the driver and stock leanchecker gave the same results on a test set, and both rejected a module that used an environment hack. Step 8 uses the stock binary. Olean hashes were recorded before replay and are re-checked after step 8.
  • [done] 8. Replay including Mathlib The build uses Mathlib's precompiled cache. leanchecker --fresh Main replays every constant, Mathlib included, removing that dependency. It exited with status 0 after 19 h 27 m in a single thread, largest process 25.9 GB resident. Afterwards the olean hashes recorded before step 7 were re-checked. All 175,187 project and 8,700 package oleans were unchanged.

Scope

A successful check shows the stated theorems follow from Lean's logic, Mathlib's definitions and Lean's standard axioms, assuming the kernel is correct. It depends on step 3: the theorems must say what they are claimed to say.

It does not give a human-readable proof or check the informal account in the announcement.
