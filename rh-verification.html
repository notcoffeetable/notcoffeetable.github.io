RH Verification

checking the Lean formalization of Alpöge's announced proof of RH/GRH · last updated 2026-11-21

Levent Alpöge has announced a proof of RH and of GRH for Dirichlet L-functions, given as a Lean 4 formalization distributed as alpo.ge/lean.tar.gz.part01–part14.

Andrew Sutherland has independently checked the same archive and reports that it passes. Separately, Alessandro Maria Masullo has circulated a human-written draft proof of GRH. Both are linked under Elsewhere.

This page contains some of my own thoughts and an independent check of Alpöge's formalization on my personal hardware: what has been checked, how, and what remains.

My own evolving thoughts

The past year has seen rapid AI adoption and considerable progress throughout the sciences, perhaps nowhere more so than in mathematics research. A confluence of modern factors now challenges practicing mathematicians: constrained department budgets, large-scale reductions in science funding, and agentic systems adept at navigating the intricate machinery of mathematical proof. For a subject that demands such high investment, the vision of a new style of mathematics is discomforting. Others have written and spoken better about the human side of mathematics (see Quanta and The Daily).

Navier–Stokes was a bellwether. But to me, an arithmetic geometer by training, a proof of GRH is akin to the moon landing. A Lean-verified proof without complete human interpretation is something else: a turn in history we watched approach for miles that nevertheless arrived sooner than some of us expected.

Evolution in our standards of proof is the history of mathematics. Euclid built geometry on axioms, while Diophantus proved by construction. In the Renaissance and early modern period, proof became laissez-faire. Tartaglia guarded the solution of the cubic as a trade secret until Cardano published it, algebra left the geometric standard of rigor behind, and results were stated without proof (Fermat's margin famously so). Algebraists rebuilt their subject on Cantor's set theory (Katz and Parshall, Taming the Unknown, 2014), and from 1900 through the 1940s Hilbert and then Bourbaki called for mathematics to return to simple axiomatic systems. An established, machine-checked proof of GRH would turn the next page. If this is the new contract of proof, then I wonder what responsibility the individual holds for verifying theorems they wish to accept.

I have been heartened to read Masullo's draft manuscript and I am excited to follow its peer review. Unpacking Alpöge's result will undoubtedly yield novel insights and techniques as well.

I'm running my own verification with an AI agent's assistance, as seems fitting for this proof. I priced out rented cloud compute, but the payoff wasn't there, so I am running it on my personal machine: AMD Ryzen 7 5700X3D (8C/16T), 32 GB RAM (WSL2 limited to 26 GB plus 48 GB swap), Ubuntu 24.04.5 on WSL2 (kernel 6.18.33.2), Windows 11, build on a PNY CS2130 500 GB SSD. Completion ETA is sometime on Monday unless I hit a memory issue.

— Dean Bisogno

Steps

  • [done] 1. Source and integrity 14 parts; checksums below. The joined archive decompresses cleanly to 175,190 files (175,187 .lean, about 17.4M lines). No paths escape the archive directory; no symlinks.
  • [done] 2. Toolchain and dependencies Lean v4.33.1 as pinned, on Linux (WSL2). Mathlib at the pinned commit 0df444a3.
  • [done] 3. Theorem statements Main.lean proves Mathlib's RiemannHypothesis and four statements in Statements.lean, which imports only Mathlib. The four: RH for ζ; L(χ, s) ≠ 0 for Re s > 1/2 and all Dirichlet χ; critical-strip zeros on Re s = 1/2 for all χ; the same for primitive χ. Each matches the standard formulation. The reduction from non-vanishing to RH/GRH uses the functional equation, the Euler factors from changing modulus, and the non-vanishing of Γℝ.
  • [done] 4. Text scan for checker bypasses No axiom, sorry/admit, native_decide/ofReduceBool, implemented_by/extern, kernel-skipping options, or notation redefining ℂ, ℝ, = or <. The three custom metaprograms build proof terms or add declarations via addDecl, so their output is kernel-checked; none adds axioms. Steps 6–7 are the definitive checks.
  • [running] 5. Full build: lake build Main All 175,187 modules. Estimated about 400 CPU-hours from 30 sampled files.
  • [pending] 6. Axiom audit #print axioms on the five main theorems, plus a census of every axiom in the imported environment. Expected: propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound.
  • [pending] 7. Kernel replay leanchecker (bundled with the toolchain) re-checks every declaration in the project's compiled output.
  • [pending] 8. Replay including Mathlib The build uses Mathlib's precompiled cache. leanchecker --fresh Main replays every constant, Mathlib included, removing that dependency.

Scope

A successful check shows the stated theorems follow from Lean's logic, Mathlib's definitions and Lean's standard axioms, assuming the kernel is correct. It depends on step 3: the theorems must say what they are claimed to say.

It does not give a human-readable proof or check the informal account in the announcement.
